# History and Credits

## Making Xargon's Revenge

I wrote Xargon's Revenge for the Commodore 16 and Plus/4 in 1985. The main source calls it `XARGON II`, credits Micro Projects and says I started on a drizzly Saturday afternoon, 30 March, at 5 pm. The cassette inlay and compilation leaflet identify Gremlin Graphics Software Limited and carry a 1985 copyright notice. I do not have a more precise completion or release date.

As I recorded on 21 October 2026, I was a fan of Scramble and wanted to make a scrolling game. I do not remember much more about its development. I used the same C64 and shared Commodore 1541 arrangement for all my C16 games: editing and assembly on the C64, then loading and testing on the C16.

The packaging describes this as a return to Xargon after Xargon Wars, with four satellite planets and the final planet to attack. That is the published setting. My Scramble recollection concerns my interest in making a scrolling game; it does not establish an official arcade conversion or a complete account of the design process.

## Credits Supported by the Material

The source's historical copyright notice is `(C) MICRO PROJECTS 1985`. Its scrolling message names Xargon's Revenge and thanks everyone who helped with production and design, without naming individuals. It also advertises other C16/Plus4 games from Gremlin Graphics. I have preserved that text and the notices in the assets and packaging.

I confirmed on 21 October 2026 that I do not remember additional contributors or their roles. I have not assigned the concept, artwork, graphics, audio or testing to anyone without evidence. The cassette artist is not identified in the supplied material. A sound-source comment mentions trying to write a routine like “our mate Robbie” does. It gives no surname or contribution to this game, so I have not turned it into a collaborator credit.

I also confirmed that the supplied images and scans are my own, made from magazines or original cassettes I own. Ownership of those physical items does not establish copyright in every reproduced image. [Artifacts](ARTIFACTS.md) identifies their contents, and [Rights and Preservation](../RIGHTS.md) keeps the distinctions clear.

## Recovery Background

The longer recovery story, development setup and recovery-tool acknowledgements are in my companion [Dorks Dilemma C16 repository](https://github.com/anthonyjclarke/Dorks_Dilemma_C16), particularly its [Development History](https://github.com/anthonyjclarke/Dorks_Dilemma_C16/blob/main/docs/DEVELOPMENT_HISTORY.md). I have not repeated that account here.

For this game I have a 1992 export and a fresh 2026 disk capture with manifests. The old `INDEX` records 7 October 1992 export timestamps; these do not date the game's development. The 2026 folder dates identify the later recovery material. I have not established an exact capture day from those labels alone.

I adapted the reassembly, VICE verification, graphics and offline-handbook approach from Tycoon Tex, which in turn builds on the Dorks Dilemma restoration. This game's source priority, memory layout and reference differences are documented in [Source and Validation](SOURCE_AND_VALIDATION.md).

## Modern Tools

I use the cc65 assembler and linker, VICE, PlatformIO, VS Code, Python and Pillow for the preservation and analysis work. The handbook embeds Marked 17.0.5 and Mermaid 10.9.3 with their licences. The original development tools remain in the recovered collections. Their presence is evidence of the surviving environment, not proof that every tool was used for every version of the game.
